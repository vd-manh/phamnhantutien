# Research the subject (step 2a)

Look the subject up on the open web before the `shape` question and before the brief's beats and carrier are
fixed. The goal is how the subject is known and how it moves, so the shapes and beats come from it.

## Tools

Use `WebSearch` to find pages and `WebFetch` to read them. When the harness has neither, fetch the DuckDuckGo
HTML endpoint (`https://html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=<query>`) over HTTP. When no route works, say so in one line
and go on from prior knowledge, labelled as such.

## Queries

Write 2 to 4 queries before running any, one per angle, specific and multi-word. Operators help: `"exact
phrase"`, `site:`, `-term`, `OR`.

- **Named move, spell or creature** (Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms, a sword art, a summon): its source work,
  how it is described or drawn there, its element, its motion, and the parts it is known by (a dragon head, nine
  rings, a lotus). Search the original-language name as well as the English one.
- **Plain effect** (fire, lightning, ice, smoke, water): how the real thing moves, breaks up and changes colour
  over time. Slow-motion footage write-ups and physics or VFX breakdowns beat generic articles.
- **Visual reference** (either kind): one query for how artists draw it, such as `<subject> pixel art effect` or
  `<subject> VFX breakdown`. Take principles only (shape language, beats, colour bands), the same rule as for an
  image the owner gives.

## Objects (dragon, sword, creature, flower)

When the effect carries an object or creature, research its structure as well as its story, so the shape is the
true one:

- **Parts and proportions.** Query the anatomy or construction: `<object> anatomy`, `<object> parts diagram`,
  `<object> reference sheet`, and the original-language name (`龙 九似`, `剑 结构`). Write the parts in order with
  their sizes relative to the whole: an Eastern dragon is a long scaled serpent body with four legs, deer antlers,
  a camel-like head with whiskers and a mane, eagle claws (count the toes the source gives); a jian is a straight
  double-edged blade about three quarters of the length, a guard, a grip and a pommel.
- **The silhouette that reads.** Which parts carry recognition at small size (the dragon's head and antlers, the
  sword's guard and tip); those get the pixels.
- **A reference image** for the skeleton step (SKILL.md 2a): a clear side or three-quarter view on a plain ground,
  from the owner, a photo, a reference sheet or public-domain art. Note its link.

## Read and check

- Open the 2 to 4 strongest results and read the page; snippets drop the detail that matters.
- Prefer the source work, wikis that cite it, and real footage over SEO lists.
- A finding that decides a shape or a beat needs 2 independent sources. Two pages copied from one origin count as
  one. When sources disagree, give both and say which one the brief follows.
- Stop at about 6 searches. What stays open goes in the reply as open.

## Report

Put 3 to 5 findings in the reply, each one line with its link, then build the brief and the `shape` options from
them. Mark anything single-sourced. Never present recall as a search result.
